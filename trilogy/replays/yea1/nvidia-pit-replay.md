# NVIDIA Gold PIT Replay

## 0｜Case Identity + PIT Cutoff

[PIT_FACT] Case: NVIDIA Corporation; replay type: Gold; PIT cutoff: `2022-11-30`.

[PIT_FACT] The decision object is NVIDIA's accelerated-computing platform as disclosed by the cutoff, before subsequent post-ChatGPT AI-related market and price outcomes.

## 1｜Evidence Boundary

[PIT_FACT] **N1 —** [NVIDIA Corporation Annual Report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 30, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581022000036/nvda-20220130.htm), issuer NVIDIA Corporation, filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on `2022-03-18`, Item 1 pp. 4–13, Item 1A pp. 14–26, and Item 7 pp. 30–39; accessed `2026-08-23`.

[PIT_FACT] **N2 —** [NVIDIA Corporation Quarterly Report on Form 10-Q for the quarter ended October 30, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581022000166/nvda-20221030.htm), issuer NVIDIA Corporation, filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on `2022-11-18`, Item 1 pp. 3–23, Item 2 pp. 24–32, and Part II Item 1A pp. 35–40; accessed `2026-08-23`.

[PIT_FACT] **N3 —** [NVIDIA CUDA Toolkit 11.8 Release Notes](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/11.8.0/cuda-toolkit-release-notes/index.html), publisher NVIDIA Corporation, last updated `2022-10-06`, Sections 1–2 including CUDA 11.8 features and CUDA Libraries release notes; accessed `2026-08-23`.

[PIT_FACT] N1–N3 were inspected as primary pre-cutoff filings, earnings-period evidence, and archived CUDA platform documentation; issuer performance and ecosystem descriptions remain issuer claims unless corroborated by filed operating figures.

[UNKNOWN] The inspected PIT corpus does not observe customers' internal total cost of ownership, application-level switching decisions, or independent measures of CUDA developer activity.

## 2｜B1 Value Space

[PIT_FACT] N1 described accelerated computing across AI, scientific computing, data science, robotics, autonomous vehicles, and graphics, with GPUs used by thousands of enterprises and available through every major server maker and major cloud provider named in the filing (N1, Item 1 pp. 4–10).

[PIT_FACT] N1 reported more than `2,500` accelerated applications, GPU and networking presence in over `70%` of TOP500 supercomputers, and engagement with thousands of organizations working on AI (N1, Item 1 pp. 5–8).

[PIT_FACT] Fiscal 2022 Data Center revenue grew `58%` year over year, and Compute & Networking revenue grew `61%`, driven in part by hyperscale cloud and workloads including natural-language processing and recommender models (N1, Item 7 pp. 31–37).

[PIT_FACT] In the quarter ended October 30, 2022, Data Center revenue grew `31%` year over year even as consolidated revenue fell `17%`, with growth across U.S. cloud providers, consumer internet companies, and other industries (N2, Item 2 pp. 27–30).

[PIT_INFERENCE] The breadth of production workloads, cloud and server availability, and pre-cutoff Data Center growth made accelerated computing and AI compute an identifiable expanding value space before post-ChatGPT outcomes.

[PIT_INFERENCE] B1 is `SUPPORTED`; the claim is about knowable workload migration toward accelerated computing, not a forecast of later demand magnitude or securities prices.

## 3｜B2 Value Density

[PIT_FACT] NVIDIA defined its offering as processors, interconnects, software, algorithms, systems, and services, with a unified architecture and multiple software stacks serving distinct markets (N1, Item 1 pp. 4–10).

[PIT_FACT] The platform included CUDA, CUDA-X libraries, APIs, SDKs, tools, domain frameworks, NGC software stacks, DGX systems, and partner reference designs; N3 separately documented CUDA 11.8 compilers and a broad library set including cuBLAS, cuFFT, cuRAND, cuSOLVER, and cuSPARSE (N1, Item 1 pp. 7–8; N3, Sections 1–2).

[PIT_FACT] N1 stated that the platform could deliver substantially lower time and power use than alternative approaches and reported almost `3 million` developers using CUDA and other NVIDIA tools (N1, Item 1 pp. 8–10).

[PIT_INFERENCE] Customer budget could flow preferentially to NVIDIA rather than generic semiconductor exposure because the purchasable outcome combined workload acceleration with mature libraries, developer skills, deployment systems, networking, and support across clouds and OEMs.

[PIT_INFERENCE] The functional account was performance and power efficiency; the higher-level value account was a lower-friction path from model or scientific code to deployed accelerated workloads.

[UNKNOWN] The filings do not independently quantify price premium, customer return on investment, or how much of preference arose from software rather than current chip performance.

[PIT_INFERENCE] B2 is `SUPPORTED`, while the relative contribution of each platform layer remains uncertain.

## 4｜B3 Value Scale

### Growth Chain

[PIT_FACT] NVIDIA used one programmable architecture across several markets, distributed through major clouds, server makers, OEMs, system builders, software vendors, and developer programs (N1, Item 1 pp. 4–11).

[PIT_FACT] Data Center growth remained positive in the October 2022 quarter while Gaming revenue fell `51%` year over year, showing that customer demand was not reducible to one end market (N2, Item 2 pp. 27–30).

[PIT_INFERENCE] A reusable architecture, installed developer knowledge, application libraries, and partner distribution could make each new supported workload and deployment route improve future demand quality.

### Replication Chain

[PIT_FACT] NVIDIA's platform strategy reused common GPU and CUDA building blocks while varying software stacks, and its partner network manufactured, integrated, sold, and deployed systems across clouds and industries (N1, Item 1 pp. 4–11).

[PIT_FACT] NVIDIA did not own wafer fabrication, assembly, testing, or packaging capacity and depended on foundries and subcontractors; by October 2022 some supply lead times had exceeded twelve months (N2, Item 2 p. 24; Part II Item 1A p. 35).

[PIT_INFERENCE] Software, reference systems, and partner channels supported system-level replication beyond founder time, but fabless scale transferred important execution and supply control to external parties.

### Compounding Chain

[PIT_FACT] Fiscal 2022 revenue was `$26.914 billion`, gross margin was `64.9%`, and operating cash flow was `$9.108 billion`, versus `$5.822 billion` in the prior year (N1, Item 7 pp. 31, 36, and 38).

[PIT_FACT] For the first nine months of fiscal 2023, operating cash flow was `$3.393 billion`; the third-quarter gross margin was `53.6%` after a `$702 million` inventory provision, and nine-month inventory provisions totaled `$2.01 billion` (N2, Item 2 pp. 29–32).

[PIT_INFERENCE] Large positive operating cash generation and high gross margin established a functioning capital asset at PIT, while inventory charges and long supply commitments showed that cash compounding was cyclical and forecasting-sensitive rather than frictionless.

[PIT_INFERENCE] B3 is `SUPPORTED`: hardware, software, systems, and partner delivery formed a scalable cashflow machine, with supply and inventory risks explicitly retained.

## 5｜B4 Value Duration

### 虚

[PIT_FACT] NVIDIA described a platform identity spanning accelerated computing, AI, graphics, and domain solutions and maintained developer training and relationships across universities and startups (N1, Item 1 pp. 4–11).

[PIT_INFERENCE] Brand and category leadership reinforced developer and buyer confidence, but filed evidence does not separate brand effect from measured performance.

### 实

[PIT_FACT] NVIDIA controlled GPU, interconnect, board, system, and software design but not semiconductor fabrication or packaging (N1, Item 1 pp. 7–13; N2, Item 2 p. 24; Part II Item 1A p. 35).

[PIT_INFERENCE] Full-stack design and system integration were supply capabilities; foundry dependence was a constraint, not a supply-control advantage.

### 入

[PIT_FACT] CUDA 11.8 exposed a programming model, compilers, tools, and multiple optimized libraries, while NVIDIA reported almost `3 million` developers using its tools (N1, Item 1 pp. 8–10; N3, Sections 1–2).

[PIT_INFERENCE] Code, libraries, trained developers, deployment tooling, and operational familiarity created switching friction beyond the chip purchase, though exact migration cost is unobserved.

### 出

[PIT_FACT] NVIDIA's GPUs were offered through major clouds and server manufacturers, and the company explicitly described a growing developer ecosystem that increased platform value (N1, Item 1 pp. 4–10).

[PIT_INFERENCE] More deployed hardware attracted software and developer investment; broader software support increased hardware utility and distribution, creating a credible ecosystem loop.

### Protection

[PIT_INFERENCE] Architecture performance, CUDA libraries, developer skills, reference systems, and distribution partnerships jointly protected demand better than any one chip feature.

### Concentration

[PIT_FACT] No customer represented `10%` or more of fiscal 2022 revenue, while one customer represented `10%` of the October 2022 quarter's revenue primarily in Compute & Networking (N1, Item 7 p. 36; N2, Item 2 p. 29).

[PIT_INFERENCE] Customer-revenue concentration is a procurement metric and does not by itself establish YEA1 value concentration.

[UNKNOWN] The inspected PIT corpus does not establish market-relative share or longitudinal concentration of accelerated-computing workloads, developer effort, or value around NVIDIA's platform.

### Self-Reinforcement

[PIT_INFERENCE] The developer-software-hardware-cloud loop was self-reinforcing at PIT because each layer increased the usefulness and distribution of the others; this conclusion does not depend on later generative-AI results.

[PIT_INFERENCE] B4 is `MIXED` overall: switching frictions and the developer-software-hardware-cloud loop support protection and self-reinforcement, while market-relative value concentration is unknown and fabrication and supply control remained weak and externally dependent.

## 6｜What Was Knowable at PIT

[PIT_INFERENCE] Before the cutoff, a disciplined reader could identify AI and accelerated computing as growing production workloads, NVIDIA as a full-stack rather than generic chip exposure, a profitable and cash-generative delivery system, and an ecosystem with path-dependent developer investment.

[PIT_INFERENCE] The same reader also knew demand forecasting, inventory, export policy, custom accelerators, AMD and Intel competition, and foundry dependence could interrupt that path.

## 7｜Hard Negative / Competing Explanation

[PIT_FACT] N2 reported consolidated revenue down `17%`, Gaming down `51%`, a material inventory provision, China export restrictions, and outstanding inventory and long-term supply obligations of `$7.02 billion` at October 30, 2022 (N2, Item 1 Note 13 p. 18; Item 2 pp. 25 and 27–29).

[PIT_INFERENCE] A competing explanation was that 2020–2021 demand had pulled forward purchases, crypto and gaming distorted the installed base, hyperscalers would substitute custom chips, and NVIDIA's software position would not offset inventory cyclicality or supply dependence.

[PIT_INFERENCE] The PIT record supports the platform thesis but does not eliminate that semiconductor-cycle explanation.

## 8｜Invalidators

[PIT_INFERENCE] B1 would weaken if accelerated workloads remained niche or shifted economically to general-purpose CPUs or customer-designed accelerators.

[PIT_INFERENCE] B2 would weaken if customers could port workloads cheaply and obtain comparable end-to-end outcomes from competing stacks.

[PIT_INFERENCE] B3 would fail if common architecture reuse stopped producing operating leverage or inventory and supply commitments repeatedly consumed operating cash.

[PIT_INFERENCE] B4 would fail if developers and clouds treated CUDA compatibility as replaceable, if export controls fragmented the ecosystem, or if external foundry bottlenecks prevented reliable delivery.

## 9｜Ex-Post Outcome (strictly separated from PIT thesis)

[EX_POST_OUTCOME] [NVIDIA Corporation Annual Report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 26, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000023/nvda-20250126.htm), issuer NVIDIA Corporation, filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on `2025-02-26`, Item 7 pp. 49–71 and financial statements pp. 80–89, reported fiscal 2025 revenue of `$130.5 billion`, Data Center revenue growth of `142%`, and operating cash flow of `$64.089 billion`; accessed `2026-08-23`.

[EX_POST_OUTCOME] These 2025 results are quarantined outcome evidence and do not upgrade the B1–B4 conclusions available on `2022-11-30`.

## 10｜YEA1 Discrimination Result

[PIT_INFERENCE] Result: B1 `SUPPORTED`; B2 `SUPPORTED`; B3 `SUPPORTED`; B4 `MIXED`; evidence status `SUPPORTED`.

[PIT_INFERENCE] The strongest discriminator was the pre-cutoff combination of a unified architecture, a documented software and library platform, broad deployment routes, positive cash generation, and developer ecosystem protection and self-reinforcement, while market-relative value concentration remained unknown and fabrication control stayed weak—not later AI revenue or market valuation.

[PIT_INFERENCE] This replay does not promote YEA1 or establish that the framework predicts subsequent outcomes.
